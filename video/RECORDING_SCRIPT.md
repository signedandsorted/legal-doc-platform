# Recording script for Josh

Record each block as one file named exactly as the heading, e.g. `video/voice/josh/will_intro.wav`.
Speak naturally at a calm pace in a quiet room; a phone voice-memo is fine (convert to .wav or .m4a -> .wav with ffmpeg).
Then run `python video/render.py --voice-dir video/voice/josh --force --only will_intro` (or leave `--only` off to redo all).
Videos without a recording keep the open-source TTS voice.

## will_intro.wav  -  Making your will

Hello, and welcome. I'm going to walk you through making your will, one step at a time.
Your will says who looks after your estate, who inherits it, and who would care for any children under eighteen.
For it to be valid, you need to be eighteen or over, make it by choice, and sign it in front of two adult witnesses, who then sign it in front of you.
If you have an old will, you can upload it later, and we'll use it to fill in names for you.
Your answers save as you go, so you can stop and come back whenever you like.

## will_about_you.wav  -  About you

First, a little about you.
Please use your full legal name, as it appears on your passport or driving licence, and tell us any other names you've used.
We'll also ask whether your permanent home is in England or Wales, and whether you own anything abroad, because that can change the advice you need.
And if you're planning to marry or form a civil partnership soon, tell us. Marriage normally cancels a will, unless the will says it was made expecting that marriage.

## will_family.wav  -  Children and guardians

Now let's talk about your children.
If you have children under eighteen, you can choose guardians to look after them if there's no surviving parent with parental responsibility.
Do ask your guardians first, and think about a back-up in case your first choice can't do it.
Please list all of your children, even if you're not leaving them anything. It helps your solicitor make sure the will is robust.

## will_executors.wav  -  Your executors

Next, your executors. These are the people who'll sort out your estate after you die.
Choose between one and four adults you trust. They can also be people who inherit from you.
It's wise to add a replacement, in case someone can't act when the time comes.
If you'd like a professional firm to act, we'll add a charging clause so they can be paid for their work.
You can also name someone to deal with your online accounts and digital files.

## will_gifts.wav  -  Gifts

This is where you leave particular gifts: an item, a sum of money, your home, or a gift to charity.
Describe each item clearly. My gold wedding ring is much better than my jewellery.
Tell us what should happen if the person dies before you.
Gifts to a UK charity are free of inheritance tax, and if ten per cent or more of your estate goes to charity, the rate on some of the rest can fall from forty to thirty-six per cent.
You can also make sure your pets are looked after, with money for their care.

## will_residue.wav  -  The rest of your estate

Your residuary estate is everything that's left after debts, funeral costs and gifts. For most people, it's the biggest part.
Give each person a share. One and one means half each.
For younger people, you can choose whether they inherit at eighteen, twenty-one or twenty-five. Until then, your trustees can use the money for their education and upkeep.
We'll also add a survivorship period, so someone has to outlive you by, say, twenty-eight days to inherit.

## will_trusts.wav  -  Trusts

Trusts can protect what you leave behind, and this is where our service goes further than most.
A life interest trust lets a partner live in your home for the rest of their life, while protecting its value for your children.
A discretionary trust lets your trustees decide who benefits and when, guided by your letter of wishes.
There are also trusts for someone who is disabled or vulnerable, and for passing on a family business.
If none of these apply, simply answer no. Your solicitor checks every trust before you sign.

## will_extras.wav  -  Extra wishes

A few extra wishes now. They're optional, but they make life much easier for your family.
Tell us where your list of online accounts is kept. Never put passwords in your will.
You can cancel loans you've made to family, and say whether big lifetime gifts should count against someone's share.
If you're leaving someone out who might expect to inherit, explain why in a separate statement. Your solicitor will review it.
And you can record your funeral and organ donation wishes, and write a private letter of wishes for your executors.

## will_documents.wav  -  Your documents

If you have an old will, codicils, or other papers, upload them here. A photo or a scan is fine.
We read the text and suggest names and details, and you choose which ones to use with a single tap.
Your new will cancels all earlier wills, but please don't destroy your old original until the new one is signed.

## will_review.wav  -  Check and send

Well done, you're nearly there.
Read through your draft. Every clause has a short, plain English note explaining what it does.
When you're happy, send it for review. A solicitor checks it and gets in touch. Nothing is signed or filed for you.
To sign, you'll need two witnesses over eighteen who can both see you sign. They mustn't inherit anything, and neither should their husband, wife or civil partner.

## lpa_property_intro.wav  -  LPA for financial decisions

Hello. Let's prepare your lasting power of attorney for financial decisions. That's the official form L P one F.
It lets people you trust run your bank accounts, pay your bills, and buy or sell your home if you ever need help.
You'll need attorneys, a certificate provider, and witnesses. Replacement attorneys and people to notify are optional, but they add protection.
Everyone signs the same original paper form. Then it's registered with the Office of the Public Guardian, which costs ninety-two pounds and takes eight to ten weeks.

## lpa_property_when.wav  -  When can they act?

Section five asks when your attorneys can make decisions.
You can let them act as soon as the LPA is registered, with your permission while you can still decide for yourself.
Or you can say they can only act when you don't have mental capacity.
Many people choose the first option because it's practical, for example if you're in hospital or can't get to the bank.

## lpa_health_intro.wav  -  LPA for health and care

Hello. Let's prepare your lasting power of attorney for health and care decisions. That's the official form L P one H.
It lets people you trust make decisions about your daily care, medical treatment, or moving into a care home, if you can't decide yourself.
You'll need attorneys, a certificate provider, and witnesses. Replacement attorneys and people to notify are optional, but they add protection.
Everyone signs the same original paper form. Then it's registered with the Office of the Public Guardian, which costs ninety-two pounds and takes eight to ten weeks.

## lpa_health_life_sustaining.wav  -  Life-sustaining treatment

Section five is about life-sustaining treatment. This is care or medicine needed to keep you alive.
Option A gives your attorneys the authority to give or refuse consent to it on your behalf.
Option B means they can't. Doctors will decide, taking your attorneys' views into account.
Take your time with this one. You'll sign this section in front of a witness, at the same time as you sign section nine.

## lpa_donor.wav  -  About you, the donor

On the form, you're called the donor. That's the person giving power to others.
You must be eighteen or over, and able to make the decision to create this LPA.
Please give your full name, date of birth and address, exactly as you'd like them to appear on the form.

## lpa_attorneys.wav  -  Your attorneys

Now choose your attorneys. These are the people who'll make decisions for you.
They must be eighteen or over. Pick people you trust and know well, such as your partner, adult children, or close friends.
You can have one attorney or several, but with a lot of attorneys, decisions can become difficult.
The form has room for four. If you add more, we'll put them on a continuation sheet for you.

## lpa_decisions.wav  -  How they decide

If you have more than one attorney, section three asks how they should make decisions.
Jointly and severally means they can act together or on their own. It's the most flexible choice.
Jointly means they must all agree on every decision. If one of them can't act, the LPA can stop working.
You can also mix the two. If you do, say exactly which decisions must be made together, because vague wording is a common reason LPAs are rejected.

## lpa_replacements.wav  -  Replacement attorneys

Replacement attorneys step in if one of your attorneys can no longer act, for example if they die or lose capacity themselves.
They're optional, but without them, your LPA might stop working when you need it most.
A replacement must be eighteen or over, and can't also be one of your main attorneys.

## lpa_notify.wav  -  People to notify

People to notify are an extra safeguard. They're told before your LPA is registered, and they can raise concerns, for example if they think you're under pressure.
You can name up to five people, but not your attorneys or replacements.
Before you register, you send each of them form L P three. They have three weeks to respond.

## lpa_preferences.wav  -  Preferences and instructions

This section is optional. It's where you tell your attorneys what matters to you.
Preferences are wishes they should think about, such as I would like to stay in my own home for as long as possible.
Instructions are rules they must follow. Keep them short and clear, because an instruction that's unworkable or unlawful can stop your LPA being registered.

## lpa_certificate_provider.wav  -  Certificate provider

Your certificate provider confirms that you understand your LPA, and that nobody is pressuring you into making it.
They must be eighteen or over, and either have known you well for at least two years, or have relevant professional skills, such as your GP or a solicitor.
Many people can't do this, including your family, your attorneys' families, your attorneys, and your partner. We'll ask you to confirm each check, and we'll spot clashes with any earlier LPA you upload.

## lpa_registration.wav  -  Registering your LPA

Your LPA can't be used until it's registered with the Office of the Public Guardian.
Either you or your attorneys apply. It costs ninety-two pounds, or one hundred and eighty-four for both types of LPA.
If you earn less than twelve thousand pounds a year, or receive certain benefits, you can apply for a reduction or exemption using form L P A one twenty.
Send the signed original form and the fee to the Office of the Public Guardian. The address is on the form, and in your pack.

## lpa_documents.wav  -  Your documents

If you already have a lasting or enduring power of attorney, upload it here.
We'll use it to fill in details, and we'll check that nobody named in it is being used as your certificate provider, because that isn't allowed.
Some older LPAs can still be registered with form L P two, and we'll tell you if that applies.

## lpa_review.wav  -  Check, print and sign

Your pack maps every answer to the right section of the official form, so copying it across is simple.
The order of signing really matters. First you sign, with a witness. Then your certificate provider. Then your attorneys and replacements, each with a witness. The applicant signs last.
Your attorneys can't witness your signature, but they can witness each other's.
Send your draft for review, and a solicitor will check it before you print and sign.

## probate_intro.wav  -  Probate helper

Hello. I'm so sorry for your loss. I'll help you work out what to do about probate, step by step.
Probate is the legal right to deal with someone's money, property and possessions after they die.
You might not need it if they only had savings, or owned everything jointly. Each bank has its own rules.
Before applying, you'll need to value the estate and check for inheritance tax. We'll then tell you whether you can apply online, or need a paper form.

## probate_deceased.wav  -  The person who died

First, some details about the person who died.
You'll need the death certificate, or an interim certificate from the coroner. A foreign certificate must be translated into English.
We'll ask whether they lived permanently in England or Wales, and about their marital status, because both affect whether you can apply online.

## probate_will.wav  -  The will and who is applying

If there's a will, the executors named in it can apply. You'll need the original will and any codicils, not a photocopy.
If the original has been lost, you may be able to use form P A thirteen, but you'll need to apply by post.
Executors who don't want to apply can reserve their power, or give it up using form P A fifteen.
If there's no will, the closest relative applies, usually the husband, wife or civil partner, and then adult children. An unmarried partner can't apply.

## probate_estate.wav  -  What they owned and owed

Now let's list what they owned and owed on the day they died.
Write to each bank or organisation with a copy of the death certificate, and ask for the value on the date of death.
For anything owned jointly, enter only their share, and tell us if it passes automatically to the other owner.
Remember to include debts and funeral costs. We'll work out the five values the probate application asks for.

## probate_tax.wav  -  Inheritance tax

Most estates don't pay inheritance tax. There's normally nothing to pay below three hundred and twenty-five thousand pounds, or if everything goes to a spouse, civil partner or charity.
Leaving a home to children or grandchildren can raise the threshold to five hundred thousand pounds, and unused allowances from a late husband, wife or civil partner can be added.
We'll check whether the estate is an excepted estate, or whether HMRC needs full details on form I H T four hundred.
Any tax is due by the end of the sixth month after the death.

## probate_documents.wav  -  Your documents

Upload copies of the will, the death certificate, bank letters, or valuations. Photos and scans are fine.
We read them and suggest details, such as names and dates, which you can add with one tap.
Keep the original will safe. If you apply, the original goes to the probate service, and HMRC only ever needs copies.

## probate_review.wav  -  Your probate plan

Here's your plan. It tells you whether you can apply online or by post, and exactly which forms you need.
It lists the five estate values, the application fee, and a checklist of what to gather.
The figures are estimates to guide you. Send your plan for review, and a solicitor will check it with you. We never file anything on your behalf.
